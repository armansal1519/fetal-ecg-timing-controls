# Maternal-residual proposal feasibility experiment

This exploratory protocol is specified before generating and scoring its new proposals. The original V4 patient results, including 2,116 inaccessible Vietnamese overlap references, are known. All ten B1 development recordings, twelve Vietnamese recordings, twelve Polish labour recordings and fifteen Italian recordings enter. Clinical transport is retrospective. Report the B1 fit and selection sections separately. No detector threshold is estimated from these outcomes.

Use only the four abdominal voltages and the sampling frequency to generate candidate times. Resample to 500 Hz. Use the project's existing abdominal maternal peak finder and subtraction of a leave-neighbourhood-out median maternal template (3–75 Hz input). Band-pass the residual at 12–45 Hz and normalize each lead by its median absolute residual over each 10 s block. Compute the second-largest absolute standardized lead sample, which requires two concordant deflections in time and accommodates different signs. Smooth with a Gaussian of standard deviation 6 ms. Find local maxima separated by 200 ms that exceed the block median plus three median absolute deviations. Use contiguous detection with piecewise block-normalized scores and the existing ±10 ms tight tolerance as the de-duplication radius against the original sealed V4 candidate bank. Peak times are the maxima of the smoothed envelope. No maternal exclusion mask, fetal template, parent timing, or reference annotation enters generation. Save raw candidate and estimated maternal times for each waveform before any scoring.

Repeat the original inclusive ±40 ms definition for fetal–maternal overlaps on valid exposure. For each record, count overlaps with no valid original-bank candidate within inclusive ±10 ms. Count how many such references can now match the residual proposals one-to-one at 10 ms. Report the number and density of new proposals, the number unmatched to all valid fetal references at 50 ms in a one-to-one assignment, and the incremental number of unmatched proposals when the union replaces the original bank. These are proposal burdens, not detector FP rates. Report aggregate counts plus individual recording rows.

Use 64 seeded label-blind time randomizations per recording. Within each valid interval, independently circularly rotate proposals inside consecutive blocks of up to 10 s, merging a final short remainder with its predecessor when possible (equal-width subdivisions). Repeat de-duplication against the original candidate bank. This controls local proposal density. A second control addresses shared maternal timing: in each valid interval map complete estimated maternal R–R cycles to other cycles by a nonzero cyclic permutation within groups of up to 24 intervals, preserving the normalized phase of each proposal; score only references/proposals in the complete shuffled cycles. Recompute the observed and randomized counts on this restricted exposure. Report their denominators, observed capture, mean null capture and proposal densities. These nulls describe coverage and coupling; maternal phase may still differ between participants and between measured versus estimated maternal timing. No null test establishes specificity of fetal electrical origin.

Primary feasibility population is Vietnam. Positive usefulness requires greater observed capture than both null means on their respective exposures, with the absolute proposal burden reported for interpretation. No final decoded rhythm, accuracy improvement or clinical operating point will be claimed from candidate availability.
